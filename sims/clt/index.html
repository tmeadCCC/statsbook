Central Limit Theorem Simulation

Goes with Section 7.3 The Central Limit Theorem

Choose a parent distribution and a sample size, then take samples. Each sample’s values drop into the parent distribution, and its mean drops into the distribution of sample means below. Watch how the sample means gather around the center and build a bell shape.

What to notice

Whatever the shape of the parent distribution, the sample means pile up around the parent mean μ, and their distribution looks more and more bell-shaped. The means are also less spread out than the parent values: their standard deviation is close to σ/√n. If you select n = 30 the distribution of sample means gets narrower and closer to normal. If the parent distribution is bell-shaped, the distribution of sample means gets closer to normal regardless of the sample size. But, if the sample size is under 30 and the parent distribution is not bell-shaped, the distribution may not ever be perfectly normal. That is the Central Limit Theorem.
