Coin Flip Simulation

Goes with Section 5.1 Basic Probability

Flip a fair coin as many times as you like. After every flip, the empirical probability of heads is the number of heads divided by the number of flips. Watch what happens to it as the number of flips grows larger and larger.

What to notice

After only a few flips, the empirical probability of heads can be anywhere from 0 to 1. As the number of flips grows, it settles closer and closer to the theoretical probability of 0.5, just as in Table 2 and Figure 5.8. This is the Law of Large Numbers. Notice that the number of heads does not have to be exactly half; it is the proportion of heads that gets closer to 0.5.
