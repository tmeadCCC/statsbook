Investigate the Margin of Error

Goes with Section 9.1 Confidence Intervals

Move the sliders for the sample size and the confidence level, and watch the confidence interval for the population mean shrink or grow as the margin of error changes.

A sample from a normally distributed population has sample mean x̄ = 50 and sample standard deviation s = 10.

Margin of error E = 7.15 Interval (42.85, 57.15) Width 14.31
Confidence interval x̄ ± EStarting interval (n = 10, 95%)

What you should notice

Sample size: as you increase the sample size n, the margin of error decreases and the confidence interval gets narrower. A smaller sample size gives a larger margin of error and a wider interval. More data gives a more precise estimate.

Confidence level: as you increase the confidence level, the margin of error increases and the confidence interval gets wider. A lower confidence level gives a smaller margin of error and a narrower interval. To be more confident that the interval captures the population mean, we need a wider net.

The interval is always centered at the point estimate x̄ = 50. Only the margin of error changes. Notice too that the margin of error shrinks quickly at first, then more slowly: going from n = 10 to n = 40 cuts it by more than half, but going from n = 70 to n = 100 changes it much less.
